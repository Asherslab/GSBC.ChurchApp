Chats (Ministries Stage 4): sketches to choose from

Two or three ways each for eight places, from the interview in docs/features/ministries.md (Chats, built well, from the first release, Asking to join is a chat, Safeguarding, for later). The owner was asleep, so Claude picked one in each; the card with the aqua border is Claude's pick (owner can redirect), and it is what the builder works from (picks.md). Every sketch uses the app's real colours, type and components; names and ministries are made up.

Underneath every option is the same: group chats and leaders-post-only channels, each for an audience (the team, attendees and their parents, parents only, chosen people); every version of every message kept; two adults with a current blue card and the parents' yes before a young person is in a chat; checks and reports logged.

1. The chats list: the app-wide list and a ministry's Chats tab

Unread, muted, waiting for a parent's yes, "you can read this" for a parent, channels and group chats.

Claude's pick (owner can redirect)

Newest first

One list, newest first, from the Hub tile; a ministry's Chats tab is the same rows for that ministry, with New chat for leaders. Each row: icon tile (speech bubble for a group chat, megaphone for a channel) in the audience's tint, the audience as a coloured word, the last message, time and the unread count. A parent's yes waiting sits on top as a warm row.

Why: chats are about what's new, and people are in two or three ministries at most; the audience word keeps the bulletin boxes' colours without the boxes.

2. A conversation

Bubbles, sending and couldn't send, replies, reactions, mentions, "Seen by", edited and deleted, photos and files, the quiet staff line, the parents line, a paused person, a channel, and a parent writing for their child.

Claude's pick (owner can redirect)

Bubbles

Others on sand at the left with their Avatar and name, yours navy at the right. The staff line is a centred caption at the very top that scrolls away. Status lives under your last bubble ("Seen by 4", "Couldn't send · Try again"). A channel or a pause replaces the composer with one plain sentence.

Why: everyone in the congregation, from teenagers to older members, already reads bubbles; who said what is clear at a glance.

3. Message actions and earlier versions

Long-press: reply, react, copy, edit, delete, report, see earlier versions.

Claude's pick (owner can redirect)

A sheet

The message lifts over the scrim; the Sheet holds a row of six reactions then action rows. Earlier versions open in a sheet, newest first, with changes marked. Delete confirms and says the church keeps a copy. Editing happens in the composer (from B).

Why: the app's own Sheet, big targets, and the same on iPhone and Android.

4. Starting a chat

Name, kind, who it's for, the two-adults check, and the parents' yes explained before the first message is sent.

Claude's pick (owner can redirect)

One sheet

Name, then Kind and Who it's for as stacked segmented controls; under them a sand line counting who's in it, the blue-card check, the "Parents say yes first" notice, and the first message, which is required when young people are in it. The button names the result ("Start the chat and ask the parents").

Why: it's the Post sheet's shape, and the warnings sit right where the choice that causes them is made.

6. Reporting, and the safety team's urgent view

Claude's pick (owner can redirect)

A sheet, then the admin panel

Report: the message, two choice cards (the ministry's leaders, or the church's safety team), an optional note. The safety team gets a time-sensitive notification and an inbox row; they act in the admin panel (§7).

Why: admin stays in the admin panel (owner, 2026-11-03), and that's where checks are logged with full context.

7. The admin panel

Claude's pick (owner can redirect)

A Chats desk with four tabs

Reports (waiting first, the message in its context, Pause, Mark as handled), Chats (the ministry tree, the chats, a deliberate "Read this chat" that's logged), Paused people, Checks log. The navy "This check is logged" line heads every reading pane.

Why: reports can't wait behind a ministry tree, and the log and pauses are church-wide.

8. Asking to join is a chat

Claude's pick (owner can redirect)

The join sheet, then the chat

Ask to join keeps the Join sheet's choice cards (who it's for) and adds the first message, prefilled "I'd like to chat about joining Choir." Sending starts a chat with the leaders. Leaders see the ask on top of that chat with Add to the team and Not now; adding leaves a line in the chat.

Why: it reuses the Join sheet, and the yes sits where the conversation is.

Open full size
Alternative

Straight into the chat

Ask to join opens the chat with the message waiting in the composer and "For me / For Mia" chips; leaders add from the chat's people sheet.

Not picked: sending is the only way out, and the who-for choice is easy to miss.
