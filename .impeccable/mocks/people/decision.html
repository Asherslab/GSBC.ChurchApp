People (Stage 1 of Ministries): sketches and what was built

Three sketches for each of the three new places: your family and blue card on the phone's Account page, the Family and Safeguarding tabs on a person's page in the admin panel, and where staff see who's flagged. The one with the aqua border is what's built tonight; the others are here so you can choose another in the morning. Every sketch uses the app's real colours and type; names are made up.

Chosen by Claude on 2026-11-10 while the owner slept, as asked. To switch, say which sketch you'd like instead; the data underneath is the same for all three, so swapping is screen work only.

1. Your family, blue card and training, on the phone

Account page in the app (and the same component on the admin panel's Account page). Who uses it: parents adding a child or making a sign-in code; young people asking a parent; volunteers checking their own card.

Built

The card in your pocket

Family as the Account page's usual rows, a waiting request first on the warm tile (as the inbox does), then your blue card drawn as a card in the masthead's navy with its number, status and expiry, and training as a row with a flag when it's due.

Why: volunteers know their blue card as a physical card, so "is mine current?" is a glance; everything else stays rows, like the rest of Account. From the household sketch it keeps the sheet for one child (the sign-in code shown big, ready to read out).

2. A person's page in the admin panel: Family and Safeguarding

Two new tabs on People › a person, beside Roles and Account. Who uses it: office staff linking a parent, adding a child for a family, recording a blue card from the card or Safe Ministry Check. Ministry leaders get the same from their team lists in Stage 2.

Built

Family in groups, safeguarding as a record

Family: the birth date, then what's waiting, then the children they look after and their own parents as groups of rows, each with an add row; a sand panel beside says what linking does. Safeguarding: a flag line when something's due, then the blue card and training as fields, with Save and a Remove for each.

Why: it reads like the Roles tab next to it (rows beside a sand explanation), each group says in words what the link means, and what's waiting comes first.

3. Who's flagged

Blue cards and training that have run out or are due, people with training but no card recorded, and young people no parent has linked. Never blocks anyone; it's a list to chase.

Built

Counts that filter one list, soonest first

A Safeguarding page under People: four counts that are also the filters, then one list ordered by when things run out, each opening the person's Safeguarding tab. Plus a line in Home's Needs you when anything has run out.

Why: one place to work through, in the order it matters; the counts answer "how are we going?" without a report. Home's Needs you comes from the second sketch.

What changed while building

  • Flagged list: the four big counts became the filter chips (each with its count) over one table, run out first then by date. Big standalone numbers would have been a report on top of the list; the chips do the same job in one row.
  • Your family: everyone is asked their age once, in a white card at the top of the section (a birth date, or "I'm 18 or older").
  • How a young person is reached (owner's decision passed on during the night): Directly, Through you, or Both on the child's sheet, and on their Family tab for staff; each child's row says how they're reached.
  • The card's label is "QLD blue card", so its status fits beside it on a phone.
Also weighed and declined (the design process deals a few outside ideas each round; none fit an established design on a working screen):
  • A boarding pass and gate board: declined, but its rule kept: a flag holds its colour until it's fixed, and the list is ordered by when things run out.
  • Metro tiles, a dance-notation score, a vertical video feed, a zoo map, a type specimen, an annual's plate section, a grid specimen, an acetate tab board, a ticket wallet: each would replace the app's look rather than serve this task.
