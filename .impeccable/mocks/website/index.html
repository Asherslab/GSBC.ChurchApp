Website embeds: how the pages are laid out

The look is settled: the church website's own fonts, greens, buttons and spacing. What's open is the layout. Three were dealt; the overnight build uses Agenda. Each card's sketch is live; open it to see it full size. The pages use made-up names and sample pictures.

Split reader

A list on the left and the chosen event or sermon open beside it, like a mail app.

  • Fast to browse many sermons on a computer.
  • Collapses to list then page on a phone, so phones get the Agenda anyway.
  • The page's height jumps as the open item changes, which a resized iframe makes visible.
Open the sketch

Shelves

Sermons as rows of series you scroll sideways; events as posters grouped by month.

  • Leads with the series artwork the church already makes.
  • Sideways scrolling hides most of each shelf, and an event without a picture becomes a blank poster.
  • Weekly services have nowhere quiet to sit.

Agenda built overnight

Events by week, each day anchored by a date tile; one-offs and specials in full, day-to-day services once under Regular gatherings. Sermons lead with the latest, then by month in the website's own rows.

  • Reads the same on a phone and a computer, top to bottom, which suits a resizing iframe.
  • Keeps the app's calendar decisions (regular events in the background, "Not on this week").
  • Closest to the pages people know, made clearer.

Also considered

Today's pages, played straight: the website's sermon rows with the filter column, and its list or calendar of events. Always available if the church prefers what it has.

Other looks (an ocean dive, a cloud quarry, a patch-bay schedule, a film bench, a magazine program) were set aside: the owner asked for the website's look.

To change the layout, say which card; the stylesheet and pages are shared, so a switch is a change to the page markup, not the look.
