4. Choosing a picture for a tile

"Choose" under Picture embedded the whole Media table in a 380px column: two scrollbars, a cut-off column, wrapping chips, and the detail panel landing on top. It's the shared chooser (MediaChooser → Media's library), also used by the rich text editor and the list editor.

Chosen
A · SHEET

A picture sheet over the workbench

Choose opens a wide sheet: search and Upload at the top, a grid of thumbnails with names, one click selects (orange ring, a tick), Choose and Cancel at the foot, double-click chooses. The place picker already works this way, and the sheet suits every caller.

7. Who sees a tile

One select listing every role, one role only. It has to scale to many roles and allow several, and still be checked through Access (a role's membership grant).

Chosen
A · THREE CHOICES, THEN ROLES

Everyone · Signed in · People in roles

A segmented choice. "People in roles" shows chosen roles as removable chips and a search field that lists matching roles as you type (arrow keys, Enter). Built once in Platform.Ui so Announcements can use it later.

5. Dragging a tile

Dragging worked, but only an outline showed where it would land. Like the iPhone's home screen, the other tiles should move aside while dragging.

Chosen
A · LIVE REFLOW

Neighbours make room as you drag

The tile lifts (slightly larger, deeper shadow) and follows the pointer; the grid repacks with the tile in its new place, a dashed orange slot where it will land, and the others glide there in 200ms. Drop commits; Escape or dropping off the phone glides everything back. Reduced motion: tiles jump, no glide. Keys: arrows move it a place, and the others glide too.

B · SWAP

Swap with the tile under you

The tile under the pointer is outlined; dropping swaps the two. Simple, but sizes differ, so a swap reshuffles the rest at the drop with no warning.

C · INSERTION LINE

A line where it goes

A line between tiles marks the insertion point; nothing moves until the drop. Precise in a list, but in a packed grid the result is hard to picture.
